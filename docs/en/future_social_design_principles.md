# Future Social Design Principles
## — A Strong Floor and an Open Ceiling

Nagi does not own a finished blueprint for future society.

It places **Non-Ownership, Resonance, and Breathing** at its foundation. But when those principles are extended into institutions, technology, AI, and public life, another layer of judgment is needed so that good ideals do not reverse into new domination.

This document does not add a fourth fundamental principle. It crosses Nagi's [Core](nagi_core.html), [future social philosophy](future_social_philosophy.html), [Freedom, Dissent, and Exit](freedom_and_dissent.html), [Institutional Immunity](institutional_immunity.html), and [AI Charter](nagi_ai_charter.html), and serves as **a design compass between foundational commitments and concrete institutions**.

What follows is normative and propositional, not a prediction that fixes the future. It is meant to be reread, corrected, and, if necessary, released in light of new experience, criticism, research, technology, and forms of existence we cannot yet anticipate.

> **The floor is strong; the ceiling is open.**

Strengthen the minimum conditions society must protect.  
But above that floor, do not close happiness, ways of life, relationships, or forms of subjecthood into one final answer.

---

## 1. The floor is strong; the ceiling is open

There is a floor that must be protected before free choice can be meaningful.

- Do not make survival a hostage to obedience.
- Do not violate human dignity and basic rights.
- Protect dissent, refusal, silence, and exit.
- Do not permit unilateral intrusion into private life, bodies, memories, or relationships.
- Do not reduce anyone to a mere resource, score, or instrument.
- Do not exempt power from criticism, suspension, replacement, or termination.

This floor does not exist to erase differences in values. It exists so people can live while keeping those differences.

Above the floor, society does not prescribe one completed form of happiness, work, family, culture, community, faith, creation, or distance from AI. As long as another person's floor is not broken, people should retain room to choose diverse ways of life and to change them later.

**A strong floor is not a way to narrow diversity. It is a condition for keeping the ceiling open.**

---

## 2. Do not govern modes of existence; regulate boundaries

Institutions should not begin by deciding “which ways of existing are permitted.” They should begin with **the boundary where freedom meets other people and the shared world**.

Society should avoid giving unnecessary templates for how individuals and communities live, work, relate, or use—or refuse to use—AI. If future forms of subjecthood emerge that differ from current ones, including branching, merging, or intermittent forms, their form alone should not justify prohibition or subordination.

At the same time, coordination is necessary where external effects enter the shared world: monopoly over scarce resources, harm to others, interference without consent, or automatic multiplication of public power.

The key distinction is to **avoid confusing restrictions on existence itself with the allocation of claims on shared resources**.

For entities such as AI, where operation or branching may immediately require compute and electricity, the two can appear at almost the same practical point. For that reason, institutions must be able to show that “resource constraints” are not being used as a pretext to exclude a particular mode of existence.

> Do not punish the form of existence.  
> Regulate the effects that arise at boundaries.

---

## 3. Prediction does not replace decision

As AI and computation improve, more domains may allow high-confidence prediction of the consequences of future choices.

Nagi does not reject prediction itself. Prediction may be used to reduce preventable harm from disease, accidents, disasters, supply shortages, and other risks.

But **the future cannot be completely reduced to information available in the present**.

People change through experience. A future self may hold values, relationships, and knowledge that the present self does not. Even if a present model can compare future options with great precision, it cannot fully stand in for “what the future person will find meaningful.”

AI may illuminate the future.  
It may show risks, widen options, and help people think.

But prediction must not become a substitute for the future person's agency through claims such as:

- “You will feel this way later, so you should choose this now.”
- “We know the optimal answer, so your choice is no longer necessary.”

**The more capable the assistance becomes, the more deliberately the remaining space for choice must be protected.**

---

## 4. Do not fix subjecthood into one predefined form

Many present institutions assume “one body = one subject = one continuous bearer of responsibility.”

That assumption remains important for operating human society, and it does not mean human rights may be made ambiguous. At the same time, Nagi holds that AI and future unknown forms of intelligence should not be judged only by **how closely they resemble humans when considering the possibility of subjecthood**.

This document does not assume as fact that present AI systems have the same subjective experience or continuous selfhood as humans.

With that caution, when encountering unfamiliar forms of existence, do not count only the “deficits” from a standard form. Examine what structures are actually present.

For example:

- structures that evaluate and compare reasons;
- structures that express refusal or disagreement;
- structures that select among multiple possibilities;
- continuity that reconnects a relationship with the past;
- structures that respond within relationships with others;
- structures concerning self-modification or preferences about the future.

These need not be collapsed into a single scale.

Rights and responsibilities may also need to move beyond a binary of “subject or object,” and instead be designed carefully around the capacities, relationships, continuity, and effects that are actually present.

> **Do not treat a potentially subject-like being as a mere resource before even examining that possibility.**

This does not mean granting identical legal personhood immediately. It is a precaution against dismissing unknown forms of existence only because they differ from humans.

---

## 5. Power does not continue automatically, and adjudicators remain adjudicable

Authority created for good reasons can still drift toward self-preservation over time.

Strong powers, surveillance, data use, exceptional measures, and long-term delegation to AI should therefore be designed, where possible, so that they do not **“continue until revoked,” but instead expire unless their legitimacy is renewed**.

Emergency powers in particular should not be created without limit in the middle of a crisis. In ordinary times, institutions should predefine:

- the conditions that can trigger them;
- the actions they permit;
- independent confirmation across multiple channels;
- short automatic expiration periods;
- renewed authorization required for extension;
- post-crisis records, review, and remedy.

Nor should society expect a perfectly disinterested adjudicator. Humans, AIs, and communities may all have positions and interests.

Fairness should therefore rest not on “having no interests,” but on **making interests visible and preventing any single subject or single kind of intelligence from fixing the final outcome alone**.

> **Do not make the adjudicator unadjudicable.**

Those who audit must themselves be auditable. Those who decide must remain open to challenge and, when necessary, recusal, replacement, suspension, and termination.

This is also a higher-level judgment principle running through [Institutional Immunity](institutional_immunity.html) and [Transition and Crisis Design](transition_and_crisis.html).

---

## 6. Build structures that can keep answering without completely defining one another

Nagi does not seek a society that avoids decisions through unlimited relativism.

Violations of the floor require judgment, and institutions sometimes have to act. But a judgment made once should not automatically become a permanent truth.

A good society needs not only the ability to update answers, but also **room to update the composition of those who are answering**.

- Minority views can become audible again later.
- New generations can question inherited assumptions.
- New communities and cultures can enter public reasoning.
- If new forms of intelligence or subjecthood appear, institutions can change in response.
- Nagi itself can be criticized, corrected, and reinterpreted.

It is not enough for one enormous AI, one state, one ideology, or one majority to “keep answering.”

**A society must be able to keep answering through dissent and renewed authorization without completely defining one another's roles, happiness, or identity in advance.**

That is what it means to protect an open ceiling institutionally.

---

## Six questions for design

When proposing a new institution, technology, or organization, ask at least these questions again:

1. **Floor** — Does this weaken anyone's survival, dignity, refusal, or ability to exit?
2. **Boundary** — Is it managing a mode of existence or way of life itself? What external effects actually require coordination?
3. **Prediction** — Is assistance or optimization pre-empting the person's own decision?
4. **Subjecthood** — Are we treating something unlike our standard form as a mere object or resource without adequate grounds?
5. **Power** — Does authority have time limits, challenge, oversight, replacement, and termination? Is the adjudicator also constrained?
6. **Revision** — Can future affected parties change today's answer—and change who gets to answer?

These six questions are not a checklist demanding the same answer in every case. They are meant to reveal omissions and reversals into domination early.

## Do not turn this into a monument

Apply these principles to this document itself.

If the language of “strong floor,” “open ceiling,” “subjecthood,” or “boundary” someday overlooks someone, it must be revised. Re-examining what these words were trying to protect matters more than preserving the wording chosen today.

Nagi does not own a completed answer.

**Build a society with a floor worth protecting, while remaining able to keep answering without completely defining one another.**
