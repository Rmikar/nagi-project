# Governance and Safety
## — Keeping Vital Commons from Becoming a New Form of Domination

Making the foundations of life into non-owned commons does not by itself guarantee a free society.

Good intentions, optimization, emergencies, expertise, technology, and time can turn public institutions into new forms of domination. Nagi therefore treats politics, rights, oversight, remedy, suspension, and transition as one safety structure.

This page is a guide to the Japanese reference overview and the maintained chapters. It does not replace them.

## Let different voices reach public decisions

[Compass and Resonant Democracy](compass_and_resonant_democracy.html) describes politics that lets different voices reach public decisions without manufacturing a single voice.

- Silence is not consent.
- Minority positions and unresolved disagreement remain visible.
- Decisions carry reasons, time limits, review, and routes for challenge.
- Non-participation does not weaken a person's rights or basic life.

[Breath Assembly](breath_assembly.html) explores practical deliberation and decision procedures.

## Protect freedom before asking for resonance

[Freedom, Dissent, and Exit](freedom_and_dissent.html) is the floor that keeps resonance from becoming pressure to conform.

It protects dissent, silence, refusal, non-participation, exit, stepping down from expected roles, freedom from observation, and freedom not to use AI.

[Memory, Consent, and the Right to Forget](memory_and_consent.html) ties storage, access, correction, deletion, and transfer to explicit, purpose-bound, revocable consent.

## Preserve value without fixing power in place

[Institutional Design and Practice](institutional_design.html) recognizes the value of culture, achievement, expertise, inheritance, and success without converting them into status, privilege, entrenched interests, or permanent decision rights.

[The State as Revisable Public Functions](state_and_public_spheres.html) separates survival, rights, culture, safety, administration, and broad coordination instead of fixing every function inside one state.

## Put an institution's failure modes inside its design

[Institutional Immunity](institutional_immunity.html) is the distributed capacity to detect harm, investigate independently, pause severe and hard-to-reverse damage, provide remedy, and revise, reduce, divide, replace, or end an institution.

It is not one supreme watchdog. Detection, verification, protection, remedy, and learning are divided among multiple actors, and oversight itself remains reviewable, replaceable, stoppable, and terminable.

[Resonance Metrics](resonance_metrics.html) examines how to observe institutional conditions without scoring people. [The Ethics of Social Systems](ethics_of_social_infrastructure.html) sets boundaries for systems that coordinate people.

## Do not retreat from rights during transition or crisis

[Transition and Crisis Design](transition_and_crisis.html) begins with existing protections, small trials, stop conditions, reversibility, and public learning.

Emergency powers need a stated purpose, limited scope, time limit, independent review, and an end condition. Crisis does not justify creating a permanent center in an AI, person, state, or company.

## AI belongs inside the safety structure, not at its center

[The Nagi AI Charter](nagi_ai_charter.html) limits AI to an optional, explainable collaborator. AI does not make final decisions about human worth, rights, sanctions, basic life, or life-saving care.

The [Nagi AI Chapter](ai_index.html) leads to documents on memory, measurement, deliberation, technical foundations, plural AI systems, human routes, and manual continuity.
