# Vital Commons
## — People and Local Institutions Remain Responsible; AI May Assist; No One Owns Them

Nagi's central institutional proposal is to move the resources and infrastructure required for life into **non-owned Vital Commons**.

> Non-Ownership of the foundations of life  
> ↓  
> Maintenance by people and local institutions, with optional support from distributed public-interest AI systems \
> ↓  
> Separation of survival from obedience to capital, state, war, ownership, and social evaluation

This proposal does not assume infinite resources. Production, maintenance, skill, energy, land, materials, logistics, and care still have real costs. What Nagi removes is the conversion of those costs into a price for the right of an individual person to remain alive.

## 1. The foundations of life are neither rewards nor merely commodities

Energy, water, staple food, health care, shelter, sanitation, communication, essential mobility and logistics, reserves, and disaster recovery are conditions from which choice becomes possible.

If access to them depends on income, employment, nationality, reputation, participation, ideological agreement, or AI use, a formally voluntary contract can still hold survival hostage.

Vital Commons establish a floor:

> Losing a competition does not remove life.  
> A period without paid work does not prevent return.  
> Refusing an order does not remove food, care, shelter, or safety.

Markets, money, companies, competition, gifts, and exchange may continue above this floor. They no longer carry the right to stop another person's survival.

## 2. No person, company, state, locality, or AI owns the commons

Non-Ownership does not mean abandonment or an ownerless object for which no one is responsible.

Roles for use, maintenance, renewal, explanation, safety, and inheritance remain explicit. People and institutions can steward equipment and resources for limited purposes and periods. Those roles do not become a right to deny basic access, demand loyalty, or convert a public function into permanent private power.

The commons are supported locally, connected across regions, and carried across generations. Responsibility is distributed; the authority to terminate another person's basic life is not.

## 3. People and local institutions remain responsible; AI may assist with equipment and circulation

People and local institutions retain responsibility for purposes, allocation principles, minimum guarantees, appeals, and remedy. Where communities choose to use it, AI may assist with forecasting demand, balancing generation and storage, monitoring water systems, planning staple production, coordinating health resources, locating failures, routing reserves, and supporting disaster recovery.

It does not score personality, belief, emotion, cooperation, productivity, or social worth. It does not decide who deserves food, care, housing, energy, or rescue.

Data collection remains limited to what equipment and circulation actually require. Individual behavior is not collected merely because it may improve optimization. Where aggregate or anonymous information is sufficient, personal records are not created.

People retain access through human, local, offline, and manual routes.

## 4. Do not make one AI the nervous system of society

Vital Commons cannot depend on one model, vendor, state, network, ledger, or control center.

They require:

- multiple AI systems with divided roles and jurisdictions;
- independent comparison and adversarial testing;
- distributed generation, production, reserves, and supply routes;
- human operators and maintained manual skills;
- offline operation and local fallback;
- the ability to isolate one dangerous function without stopping the whole system;
- transparent incident records and supply-first remedy.

Redundancy is not waste to be optimized away. It is part of the material cost of freedom and continuity.

## 5. Energy and water do not become weapons of obedience

Energy and water systems shape every other part of life. Their ownership can turn price, disconnection, blockade, or territorial control into a direct command over people.

Nagi therefore separates the right to basic access from payment capacity, political loyalty, and status. Local systems diversify generation, storage, purification, repair, and supply routes while connecting to wider mutual aid.

Usage and scarcity still require observation. The observed object is flow, equipment, reserves, loss, and ecological capacity—not a moral ranking of individuals.

## 6. People and communities sustain staple food systems; AI and automation may assist

People and local institutions remain responsible for staple food systems. Where they are useful and chosen, automated systems may support soil and weather observation, staple crops, storage, distribution, and recovery from harvest failure. That does not reduce food to calories or replace growers, fishers, cooks, craftspeople, local knowledge, taste, ritual, and seasonal memory.

Staple nutrition belongs to the floor of life. Human food-making remains a field of culture, care, skill, experimentation, pleasure, and exchange.

Automation frees food culture from the threat of starvation; it does not erase the people who carry it.

## 7. Health care can be widely automated without taking bodily sovereignty

AI, robotics, diagnostics, logistics, and remote support may expand access to health care. Life-saving treatment does not depend on wealth, productivity, nationality, reputation, participation, or consent to broad data use.

People retain bodily consent, explanation, a human consultation route, a second opinion, refusal where refusal is possible, and a way to challenge harmful decisions.

AI does not make the final decision to deny life-saving care. Scarcity is handled through publicly visible criteria, independent review, urgent appeal, and later remedy—not hidden scoring of human value.

## 8. Separate survival from capital

Capital governs not only through accumulated money but through the condition that refusing work or authority may remove food, housing, care, and safety.

When Vital Commons are separated from employment and markets, companies and capital lose the power to stop basic life. Paid work, enterprise, competition, investment, and trade may still support culture, technology, preference, and additional choice.

Nagi does not abolish economic activity. It ends survival as leverage inside that activity.

## 9. Human work moves from obligation toward culture

Optional automation of selected foundational production does not make human activity unnecessary.

People may farm, build, repair, teach, research, care, cook, design, write, perform, and invent because they want to deepen life—not because refusal means destitution.

Some maintenance will continue to require difficult and necessary human labor. Nagi does not hide that labor. It reduces danger, shares burden, recognizes expertise, maintains routes for refusal and rotation, and does not justify coercion by romanticizing contribution.

## 10. Loosen the material conditions that make war rational

Vital Commons alone do not eliminate domination, fear, revenge, nationalism, ethnic or religious conflict, or concentrated power.

They can nevertheless weaken major material conditions that have made war rational:

- conquest for energy, water, land, minerals, or logistics;
- blockade and supply interruption as instruments of obedience;
- recruitment made possible by poverty and lack of alternatives;
- expansion required to maintain growth or control markets;
- the seizure of infrastructure as a means of governing a population.

Nagi aims to remove structures in which war is necessary for survival and structures in which war can seize the foundations of another population's life. Peace becomes material infrastructure, not only goodwill.

## 11. Survival continues when systems fail

A system for basic life must be judged under failure, attack, disaster, corruption, and misuse—not only under ideal operation.

Vital Commons therefore preserve distributed reserves, spare parts, manual operation, local authority to restore supply, multiple communication routes, independent incident investigation, and emergency mutual aid across regions.

When failure occurs, restoring water, energy, food, shelter, communication, and care comes before assigning blame. Investigation, accountability, compensation, and institutional change follow without hiding the incident.

The system that oversees these safeguards is itself subject to [Institutional Immunity](institutional_immunity.html).

## 12. Transition begins without destroying existing protections

Vital Commons are not a plan to abolish current welfare, public services, employment, or industry overnight.

Transition begins with bounded experiments:

1. identify one foundation of life and its actual material limits;
2. add an unconditional access route without removing existing protection;
3. maintain AI-assisted, human, local, offline, and manual modes in parallel;
4. publish costs, failures, exclusions, ecological effects, and conflicts of interest;
5. define pause, rollback, remedy, and termination before expansion;
6. expand only when affected people can challenge the system and leave without losing basic life.

→ [Transition and Crisis Design](transition_and_crisis.html)

## Material conditions and unresolved limits

Non-Ownership cannot create food, energy, medicine, or habitable land that does not exist.

Nagi distinguishes two conditions:

> **Non-Ownership is the political condition.**  
> **Sufficient and renewable capacity in the foundations of life is the material condition.**

Technology, repair, reuse, circulation, reserves, ecological restoration, and mutual aid can increase that capacity. Population, consumption, climate disruption, ecosystems, disaster, and material scarcity can reduce it.

When shortages remain after monopoly, waste, excessive occupation, and non-basic mass consumption have been addressed, Nagi does not hide the shortage or let AI rank people by worth. The facts, criteria, responsibility, dissent, emergency appeal, and burden of the decision remain public and reviewable.

This chapter offers a direction and safeguards, not a completed global engineering, legal, fiscal, or geopolitical system.

## Closing

Vital Commons do not promise a world without material limits. They change who may use those limits as a weapon.

> The foundations of life are cared for by many and owned by none.  
> AI may support their equipment and circulation without managing people.  
> Survival is no longer the price of obedience.
