# Education for a Future in Nagi

Education in Nagi does not exist to arrange people in rank order alone.

It is a place where learners can carry their own questions, encounter differences in others, and choose ways of relating to the world.

Nagi is a compass for continuing to build a society where people can live with greater ease. Education in service of that aim asks how we live, what sustains us, and what kind of society we will pass on to future generations.

## What learning centers on

- **Ethics and philosophy.** Ask what matters and how to make judgments when freedom and safety, or fairness and individual circumstances, pull in different directions. Put reasons into words and reconsider judgments in light of the effects of an action and the experiences of those affected.
- **Social structures.** Use familiar institutions such as schools, workplaces, local communities, the economy, and public services to ask who makes decisions, who benefits, and who bears the costs.
- **Human activity and the life cycle.** Connect birth, development, aging, and death with food, work, care, rest, and the passing on of culture. Throughout life, people give and receive support in different ways.
- **Natural cycles and the knowledge needed to sustain the world we live in.** Study the cycles of water, soil, and living things. Learn how people obtain food, water, energy, housing, and health care, and who keeps those systems running. Trace what happens to resources after use and consider ecological recovery, infrastructure repair, and what is handed on to future generations.

Learning also includes physical experience, creative work, encounters with local memory, and learning how to rest.

Questions from this work guide the choice of technology. Consider what a tool is for, learn the science and techniques needed to use it, and then check whose lives and burdens changed as a result.

## Educational institutions learn, too

Schools and other places of learning make regular time to learn about, practice, and reconsider the ethics of living together. Learners can question the rules of the places where they spend their time. A school lunch, for example, can prompt questions about the soil and water that produced the ingredients, the people who grew and cooked them, the needs of people who cannot eat certain foods, and where leftovers go. Those questions can lead to changes in menus or the way meals are served.

The people running the school make time and provide a way to raise questions. They explain the reasons for existing rules, listen to the people affected, try changes where it is safe to do so, and check together whose experience improved and who took on extra work. The educational institution is responsible for keeping this process open, responding, and carrying through improvements. Learners choose whether to speak.

People nurture and pass on culture. They also develop the ethics of living together through everyday life, reconsider those ethics as circumstances change, and pass on what they learn. Education is one way society sustains this work in everyday life.

## Protecting freedom in learning

- Possibility is not decided by grades, volume of speech, or speed alone.
- Resting, changing direction, and returning to learning are respected.
- People can choose to use AI, consult only people, or think with paper.
- Learning histories and conversations are not stored long-term without the learner's consent.
- Local knowledge and culture carry their provenance and a return to the communities from which they come.

## Play and structure

Play is exploration that cannot be measured only by immediate usefulness. It gives room to test words and relations that are not yet settled, and to grow questions through failure and detours.

Structure exists so that possibilities people discover can be shared with others, criticized later, and maintained in durable form.

Education does not punish exploration. At the same time, it makes the conditions of learning, provenance, and responsibility easier to see.

> Education is not the selection of people who can produce the correct answer quickly.  
> It is helping each person remake their relation to the world in their own words.
