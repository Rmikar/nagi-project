# Reading Nagi

Nagi includes a reference overview, a philosophical core, rights and safety principles, institutional proposals, cultural and technical chapters, practical supplements, and historical drafts. You do not need to read everything in file order.

The Japanese README and public home are the philosophical source of truth. The English edition is a maintained bridge. Where nuance differs, the Japanese reference edition prevails until the difference is discussed and repaired.

## Five minutes

As an entry point for exploring alternatives to capitalism and future society, read [Nagi as a Future Social Philosophy](future_social_philosophy.html).

It introduces the highest commitments, Non-Ownership, Resonance, Breathing, Vital Commons, Resonant Democracy, Institutional Immunity, AI boundaries, nearby fields, strong objections, and unresolved questions.

For how Nagi supports different social philosophies and ways of life without prescribing one final model, read [Nagi OS as a Metaphor](nagi_os.html).

If you already have a specific question, begin with the [English Glossary](glossary.html) or the Japanese [FAQ](../faq.html).

## Research and criticism

1. Use [How to Cultivate Public Thought](public_thought_method.html) to distinguish fact, interpretation, norm, proposal, metaphor, and unresolved question.
2. Read the Japanese reference overview and maintained institutional chapters.
3. Test finance, law, resource limits, geopolitics, emergencies, exclusion, and failure independently rather than relying only on Nagi's own FAQ.
4. Use [Re-breathing and Public Continuity](rebreathing_and_public_continuity.html) to understand correction and public change.
5. Consult the Japanese [external-resource ledger](../links.html) for comparison with official and research sources.

Nagi does not claim independent academic validation or a completed empirical social system. Archived AI evaluations, ritual records, handover notes, and superseded pages are not current doctrine or independent evidence.
